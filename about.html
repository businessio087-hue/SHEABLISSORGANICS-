02 / Our trusted supplier

Kahangi
Estate.

Natural Products, Made Naturally

At the foot of the Rwenzori Mountains in Western Uganda sits Kahangi Estate, a working farm with more than a century of stories in its soil. Founded in 1916 as a coffee estate, it became a tea estate in the 1930s. It was then left to rest in the 1980s, until a new chapter began in 2000, when the land was lovingly brought back to life.

Today, Kahangi grows green tea, Arabica coffee and a rich variety of oil crops, with no genetically modified seeds. Everything is processed and packed on the estate itself, so every product can be traced from seed to jar, and from jar to skin.

The estate has no mains electricity, so it learned to make its own. About 70% of the energy used to process and pack its products comes from the sun. The rest comes from biodiesel pressed from candle nut trees that also shelter the fields as windbreaks. Leftovers from processing return to the land to feed the next harvest, and the estate has shared its farming methods with more than 800 students from across East and Central Africa.

Kahangi borders Kibale National Park, 760 km² of ancient rainforest. It is home to chimpanzees, elephants and hundreds of bird species. Protecting that forest guides every choice the farm makes, from keeping its papyrus swamps wild to working with neighbouring farmers on chemical-free growing.

03 / The land and people of Tooro

Place gives the work
its character.

The People and Their Culture

Tooro is one of Uganda’s recognised traditional kingdoms, centred on Fort Portal City. The Batooro people speak Rutooro and are known for a strong sense of hospitality, community and royal heritage. The kingdom is led culturally by the Omukama, and its traditions continue through ceremonies, music, dance, oral history and clan relationships.

Daily life remains closely connected to the land. Bananas, millet, beans, sweet potatoes, vegetables, milk and other locally produced foods have long supported families, trade and cooperation. Handwoven baskets, bark cloth and storytelling carry skills and memory from one generation to the next.

The Ecosystem

Tooro is surrounded by rolling hills, crater lakes, forests, fertile farmland and the Rwenzori Mountains, the legendary “Mountains of the Moon”. Nearby Kibale National Park is a protected forest ecosystem in Kabarole District. Uganda Wildlife Authority records 13 primate species, more than 120 other mammal species, forest elephants and approximately 370 bird species there.

The forest’s chimpanzees, birds and other wildlife are part of a living landscape that makes conservation central to the region’s future. Rather than stating a specific elephant migration season, we describe them here as part of Kibale’s protected forest habitat.

04 / Weather patterns

A climate that
keeps giving.

Although Tooro lies near the equator, Fort Portal’s elevation gives the region a mild highland climate, with warm days and cooler evenings. Regional climate data shows rainfall across much of the year, with wetter conditions generally extending from around March into December. November is among the wettest months, while January is typically the driest.

Local travel sources often describe March to May and September to November as the principal rainy periods, with drier spells around December to February and June to August. These rhythms help shape the region’s fertile soils and the character of its tea and coffee.
